About me

From research to practice

I'm a computer scientist specialising in natural language processing, large language models and the Semantic Web. During my doctorate at the Karlsruhe Institute of Technology (KIT), I developed modular, knowledge-graph-agnostic methods for entity linking (recognising which real-world thing a word in a text refers to) and released them as open-source frameworks.

Alongside research, I spent about seven years leading and delivering applied AI projects with industry partners: from LLM-based policy compliance checking at DATEV to a network-planning tool in use at atesio. Before that, I worked as a full-stack developer at ING Luxembourg, where my tools replaced commercial software costing EUR 150k per year in licences.

I enjoy bringing technology, business units and management together. For me, a project is only finished when its results are actually used. I'm now looking for a role where I can lead AI projects like that.

Research & teaching

Research

Doctoral thesis

Modular and Adaptive Named Entity Linking

Karlsruhe Institute of Technology · submitted 07/2026 · defense on 10 Dec 2026 · supervisor: Prof. Dr. York Sure-Vetter · second reviewer: Dr.-Ing. Tobias Käfer

Entity linking connects mentions in text (such as "Paris", "Jaguar" or "Mercury") to the right entry in a knowledge base. My thesis makes this process modular (systems and components can be combined and swapped), knowledge-graph-agnostic (it works with Wikidata, DBpedia or a company's own knowledge base) and adaptive (robust LLM-based disambiguation that avoids hallucinations).
